# race-and-gender-bias-in-the-workplace

## Research Question
For the project, I had to create a survey with one element that I could change to see how it affected the results. The element I chose to study was the race and gender of a professional, and how it affected what someone thought of them.

## Methodology
I created a survey in which participants first read an article, then answered three questions. The article presented the “Long Island Surface Water Group” chairperson’s plan to prepare for harsh weather. The chairperson’s identity was changed four times to create four different articles including either a black chairwoman, a black chairman, a white chairwoman, or a white chairman. Participants were put into four groups, and each group was randomly shown only one article. After reading the article, participants ranked the competence, trustworthiness, and ability of the chairperson they read about.

I used this data to compare how a chairperson’s rank changed given their identity, and the identity of the participant. First, I analyzed how the participants’ own race and gender could influence what they believed about another person. I made two groups: one of women participants and one of men participants. Then I compared the rank of each chairperson when women took the survey versus when men took the survey. I also looked at how a participants’ political party influenced what they believed about another person. I again made two groups: Democrats and Republicans. Then I compared the rank of each chairperson for Republican respondents versus Democratic respondents.

## Results
I found that women consistently ranked the white woman as less able, competent, and trustworthy even though no facts in the article were changed. This may suggest that women are more critical of white women than any man, or than black women.
I also found that Republicans consistently ranked black and white women as less competent, able, and trustworthy than either man without any other facts of the article changing. This suggests that Republicans are more critical of women than they are of men.

## Impact
	At the time of this research, discussion of DEI policy was increasingly popular. This research aligns with other studies that suggest that people face discrimination based on their race and gender. Using this information, we may better understand the need for DEI policy and predict the negative effects of removing it.
